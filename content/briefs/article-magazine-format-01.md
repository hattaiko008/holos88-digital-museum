# HOLOS 88 ARTICLE MAGAZINE FORMAT 01

Status: approved ECHO long-form standard, 2026-09-29  
Reference implementations: `archaeology-of-fear.html` and `cabinet.html` — hachico publication approved, 2026-09-29

## Applies to

This information architecture is the default for every published HOLOS 88 article, including ECHO, TEAM HOLOS, SHE CREATES, WATCH THE NOW, letters and hachico essays. Each series keeps its own colour, typography, density and voice. Short notes and first-person essays may use a lighter expression, but the publication sequence and access rules remain shared.

## Purpose

An article is a reading space, not a column of text with pictures added afterward. Typography, factual images, diagrams, questions, captions, margins and source notes must each move the reader's understanding forward.

## Page rhythm

1. Begin with a title whose promise matches the full article, followed by one concrete subtitle.
2. Alternate sustained reading with a visual or thinking device after roughly two to four paragraphs.
3. Use section titles as meaning-bearing phrases. Do not place decorative sequence numbers above them.
4. End by returning the subject to the reader through one open question; do not force a moral.

## Editorial devices

- **Documentary plate:** a rights-cleared photograph or historical image. It must have creator, title/subject, date, institution/source, license and a precise caption.
- **Diagram:** clarifies structure, distance, chronology or relationship. Label interpretation and uncertainty.
- **Question field:** interrupts certainty and gives the reader a method for looking. It is part of the argument, not decoration.
- **Evidence desk:** separates confirmed fact, recorded evidence, interpretation, hypothesis, disputed claim and unknown.
- **Specimen card:** gives a person, object, species, place or term a stable identity and a path into the collection.
- **Collection shelf:** internal links open a browsable shelf of related words, objects and articles. Do not send readers to an unfinished standalone object page.
- **Collection card:** create a reusable visual card from an article's symbolic object, person, place or term. Use one coherent print language across the collection while naming the underlying source and any visual treatment.
- **Source desk:** lists the principal sources used for verification. Claims remain linked to their sources in structured content.
- **Reading shelf:** where books materially shaped the article, add a compact shelf with title, author, editorial reason and a stable retailer or publisher link. Avoid implying that a search link identifies one exact edition.
- **Closing bridge:** when it belongs to the essay, return the subject to the making of HOLOS and open no more than three precise onward paths. It should complete the argument before it recommends another article.

## Standard publication sequence

1. Article body and its visual or thinking devices.
2. **Visual Collection:** link article-born plates or symbolic objects to stable Museum collection cards. Omit the block when no rights-cleared, meaningful visual has been collected; never add filler.
3. **Related Reading:** two to four books that extend the article's central questions. Label these as related reading, not direct sources. Prefer stable publisher pages or clearly labelled ordinary retailer searches.
4. **Source Desk:** primary and authoritative sources used to verify the article. Keep this separate from Related Reading.
5. **Next Window:** no more than three published onward paths. Do not link a public reader into drafts, paid material without a gate, or unavailable object pages.

Visual Collection and Related Reading should feel like part of the article's editorial conclusion. Their design can change with each article's temperature, while their labels, access metadata and relationship roles stay consistent.

## Captions

Write captions in three layers: a short English plate label; a Japanese sentence explaining why the image is here; a compact rights line. State clearly when an image is illustrative, historical, reconstructed, AI-generated or not contemporary. A caption must never imply that a reference image depicts the specific event when it does not.

## Visual rules

- Let the subject determine the visual language; reuse the information architecture rather than copying decoration.
- Preserve generous reading space. Full-bleed and offset elements are deliberate pauses.
- Use one article accent colour for section titles and thinking devices, with accessible contrast.
- Check every foreground/background pair after the shared template and article-specific styles are combined. Dark fields use sufficiently light type; pale fields use sufficiently dark type. Review hero titles, subtitles, pull quotes, captions, labels and links in their rendered state rather than judging colour values separately.
- Keep title, subtitle, captions and all primary questions readable at mobile widths without clipping.
- Keep ordinary article prose at one steady weight. Do not bold a sentence merely to raise its emotional volume; create emphasis through sequence, spacing, section structure or a clearly designed editorial device. Reserve bold weight for headings, labels, diagram keys and navigational titles.
- Decorative numerals, empty labels and images without an editorial purpose are omitted.

## Completion check

- Title answers “why this title?” after reading the whole article.
- Every image has a purpose, alt text, caption and rights status.
- Fact, interpretation and symbolic lens are visibly distinguishable.
- Internal links lead to a finished page or the collection shelf.
- A symbolic object has a stable Collection ID and a reciprocal article link when it can support future relationships.
- Retail links state whether they are ordinary or affiliate links. If affiliate links are introduced, disclose them clearly and state that part of the proceeds is donated; publish the recipient and percentage once decided.
- Desktop and mobile layouts have been visually reviewed.
- No text disappears into its background through low contrast, overlays, inherited colour or later CSS overrides.
- Article remains in editorial preview until text, sources, design and rights are all approved.

## Language editions

Use `bilingual-site-structure-01.md`. Japanese and English are two editions of the same article and collection record, sharing one stable identity, relationships, media rights and publication state. Enable a language switch only after the corresponding text has completed editorial review.
